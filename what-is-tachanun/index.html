What is Tachanun?

What is Tachanun?

Tachanun (תחנון, “supplication”) is a prayer of confession and pleading said after the Amidah (Shemoneh Esrei) at Shacharis and Mincha on ordinary weekdays. Its heart is Nefilas Apayim (נפילת אפים), “falling on the face”: sitting and resting one’s head on the arm while saying a psalm of supplication, as Moshe and Aharon fell on their faces in prayer.

In Nusach Chabad (Nusach Ari), Tachanun begins with Vidui (Ashamnu) and the Thirteen Attributes of Mercy, followed by Nefilas Apayim with Psalm 6, and ends with Shomer Yisrael. On Mondays and Thursdays the longer passage Vehu Rachum is added at Shacharis.

When is Tachanun said?

Tachanun is said at Shacharis and Mincha on weekdays. It is never said at Maariv, and never on Shabbos or Yom Tov.

Because Tachanun is a prayer of contrition, it is left out on days with a festive character. These include Rosh Chodesh, Chanukah, Purim, Tu B’Shevat, the whole month of Nissan, Lag BaOmer, the first days of Sivan, Tisha B’Av, Tu B’Av and most of Tishrei. Chabad custom adds the Chassidic festivals of liberation: Yud Kislev, Yud Tes Kislev and Chof Kislev, and Yud Beis–Yud Gimmel Tammuz.

Mincha the day before

For many of these days, the joy reaches back to the afternoon before, so Tachanun is also left out at Mincha of the previous day. Examples are Erev Rosh Chodesh, Erev Chanukah (24 Kislev), 13 Adar before Purim, 14 Shevat, 17 Iyar before Lag BaOmer, 8 Av and 14 Av.

In Chabad custom, Tachanun is also left out at Mincha on Friday afternoon.

Other times Tachanun is not said

Some reasons to leave out Tachanun depend on who is present, not on the date. For example, it is not said in a house of mourning during shiva, when a chassan during his week of sheva brachos is in the minyan, or when the father, sandek or mohel of a bris that day is present. Ask your Rav about any particular case.

On days without Tachanun, the related passages are usually left out too: Lamnatzeach (Psalm 20) after Ashrei at Shacharis, and on Mondays and Thursdays Kel Erech Apayim before the Torah reading.

Krias Shema al HaMitah (the bedtime Shema)

The bedtime Shema follows the same calendar. On a night before a day without Tachanun, part of it is skipped, while Ribono Shel Olam and Lamnatzeach Mizmor L’Dovid are still said. On the night after a no-Tachanun day, the omission lasts until halachic midnight (chatzos).

Chabad custom and other communities

This site follows Chabad-Lubavitch custom, based on Chabad.org and Shulchan Aruch HaRav. Most of the dates are shared by every community. The main Chabad additions are the Chassidic festivals and the longer stretches in Tishrei (9 Tishrei through Rosh Chodesh Cheshvan) and Sivan (through 12 Sivan).

It follows the Diaspora calendar and does not include Eretz Yisrael-specific days or Yom Ha’atzmaut and Yom Yerushalayim, which some communities observe. It is a tool for checking the schedule, not a replacement for a Luach or a Rav.
