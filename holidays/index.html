The days without Tachanun

  • Shabbos, beginning from Friday Mincha.
  • Rosh Chodesh, beginning from Mincha of the day before.
  • Rosh Hashanah.
  • From 9 Tishrei through 1 Cheshvan.
  • Chanukah, beginning from 24 Kislev at Mincha through the end of Chanukah.
  • 15 Shevat, beginning from the afternoon before.
  • From 13 Adar at Mincha through 15 Adar.
  • 14 Adar I, Purim Katan.
  • 15 Adar I, Shushan Purim Katan.
  • Entire month of Nisan.
  • Issru Chag after Pesach, Shavuos, and Sukkot.
  • 14 Iyar, Pesach Sheni.
  • 18 Iyar, Lag BaOmer, beginning from the afternoon before.
  • Shavuos (Sivan 6-7).
  • Rosh Chodesh Sivan - 12 Sivan (Sivan 1-12).
  • 9 Av, beginning from the afternoon before (Sunday 10 Av when the fast is postponed from Shabbos).
  • 15 Av, beginning from the afternoon before.
  • Erev Rosh Hashana.
  • 10 Kislev, beginning from the afternoon before. Chabad
  • Yud Tes Kislev, beginning from the afternoon before. Chabad
  • Chof Kislev, beginning from the afternoon before. Chabad
  • Yud Beis Tammuz, beginning from the afternoon before. Chabad
  • Yud Gimmel Tammuz. Chabad

Days where custom varies

  • 3 Tammuz: Public custom is generally to say Tachanun, though some individuals may omit privately for personal reasons. Tachanun is omitted on days when significant joy is felt.
  • 18 Elul: Some mention omitting Tachanun, but in the Rebbe’s minyan Tachanun was said.
  • 5 Teves: Public custom is generally to say Tachanun, though a person who truly feels exceptional joy may have a personal practice.
