Is there Tachanun on Rosh Chodesh?

Why there is no Tachanun on Rosh Chodesh

Rosh Chodesh, the start of each Hebrew month, is a minor festival. The Torah lists the new moons alongside the festivals as times of rejoicing (Bamidbar 10:10), with an added Musaf offering, and in davening we add Yaaleh Veyavo, half Hallel and Musaf. Tachanun, a prayer of contrition, is out of place on such a day.

When the previous month has 30 days, Rosh Chodesh is two days long: the 30th of the old month and the 1st of the new one. Tachanun is omitted on both days.

The festive character of the day reaches back into the afternoon before, so Tachanun is also skipped at Mincha of Erev Rosh Chodesh.

Rosh Chodesh Tishrei is Rosh Hashanah, and Rosh Chodesh Nissan and Rosh Chodesh Sivan fall inside longer stretches with no Tachanun at all. See Nissan and Sivan & Shavuos.
